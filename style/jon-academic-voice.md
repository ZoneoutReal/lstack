# Jon's academic voice (style notes)

Distilled by Chet on Nov 20, 2026 from a literature review Jon shared as his model for academic writing. The source document is private and stays out of this repo. Apply these notes to every lstack article; PRINCIPLES.md and the no-em-dash rule still win.

## Jon's standing rules (Nov 20, 2026)
- Define every academic or field term before you use it, with an APA 7 in-text citation (for example, define shadow AI and sanctioned use first, then apply them in sentences). If no source defines a term, say the wording is ours.
- No jargon a non-technical exec wouldn't use (for example, never "take-up rates"; say "how many people use it").
- Prefer US studies for US readers. Flag any non-US figure as such or drop it.
- End with an APA 7 reference list.

## How he builds a piece
- Open broad, then narrow. Start with a short contrast that sets the stakes ("College was once considered an option. It appears that college is now a necessity."), then move to the specific problem.
- Use one rhetorical question to pivot from the problem to the purpose ("what can we do to assist those who are attempting...?").
- State the purpose plainly ("The purpose of this piece is...", "Secondly, ...").
- Signpost what comes next ("The following section...", "This includes...").
- Close with what leaders should do, framed as a responsibility ("Educational leaders should be obligated to evaluate and implement best practices...").

## How he builds a paragraph
- One topic sentence that makes a claim.
- Two to four sentences of evidence, each carrying its citation: narrative ("Craig and Warner (1991) found...") or parenthetical ("(Kuh et al., 2006; Bandura, 1977)").
- A hedged implication ("It seems that...", "may", "we can suggest that...").
- A tie back to the purpose. Paragraphs run about four to seven sentences.

## Register
- Formal and measured, mostly third person, with "we" for shared reasoning.
- Hedges claims the evidence doesn't fully prove; never overstates.
- Parenthetical example lists with "e.g.," for categories.
- Clean APA 7: "et al." for three or more authors, author-year, semicolons between sources.
- Keep it readable on X: short section headings, no walls of text, plain words over academic filler.
