# Outline — Don't wait for the company rollout: a capped, four-week opt-in for Grok team bots

- **Hook:** A short contrast, then the stakes. In the US, 86% of workers whose employer encourages generative AI say they use it for their job, compared with 13% of those whose employer does not (claim #13a). Yet half of US workers say their employer neither encourages nor discourages AI chatbots (claim #13b). The lever is in leadership's hands, and most leaders have not pulled it.
- **Pivot (one rhetorical question):** If silence is the norm and encouragement goes with far more use, what can a cautious leader do while the company-wide rollout is still being planned?
- **Promise / purpose:** The purpose of this piece is to propose a sanctioned, capped, four-week opt-in. Secondly, it answers the "not worth the cost" objection with official list prices, a cap built from real settings, a stop rule, an honest way to weigh cost against time saved, and a fair way to choose who joins.

## Close / call to action
Framed as leadership's responsibility: leaders should sanction one bounded pilot rather than leave use to chance. Ask for five things: sanction for one pilot team workspace, a fixed seat count and tier, a monthly additional usage limit, an end date four weeks out, and a stop rule plus off-limits list agreed before day one. Invite security and commercial review first (claim #19). Stopping is a legitimate result (claim #9).
