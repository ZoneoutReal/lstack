# Outline — Don't wait for the company rollout: sanction a capped, four-week opt-in pilot of AI agents

This pass applies Direction update 6 (01-topic.md, 0dbcb47) and Addendum 7 (02-brief.md, 24118bc). It is built on Edie's 04-final at daff1c0, with the agent definition from Addendum 6.

The rule: AI-specific data should be after May 20, 2026. The new sources and the older-data exceptions named in Addendum 7 stay with their date labels. Definitions and non-AI points are exempt, so Puthal et al., CAISI, GAO and the GOV.UK discovery guide stay. The article is tool-agnostic and has no Grok Bot. Word count: 2,377 as X counts (table rows and URLs left out, references included).

- **Hook:** manager support goes with use. Employees who strongly agree their manager supports AI use are "1.7x as likely to use AI a few times a week or more" (Gallup, n.d.; data as of May 2026). Only about a quarter of US employees, 26% as of August 2026, say their organization has communicated a clear AI plan. That figure measures a plan, not encouragement.
- **Pivot (one rhetorical question):** If few organizations have a clear plan, and support goes with more use, what can a cautious leader do while the company-wide rollout is still being planned?
- **Promise / purpose:** "The purpose of this article is" to make the case for sanctioning a capped, four-week opt-in pilot of AI agents across several uses. Secondly, it shows that results differ by use, why tasks should be timed rather than estimated, what the evidence does not yet cover, and a stop rule in plain words.

## Sections
1. **Three terms** (definitions, exempt from the recency rule)
   - Shadow AI: Puthal et al. (2025).
   - Sanctioned use: our wording, built by contrast with Puthal et al.
   - AI agent: the CAISI (2026) and GAO (2025) sentence from Addendum 6, plus GAO's "no universally agreed upon definition".
   - Ending in Addendum 7's wording: the Gallup surveys measure manager support and whether a clear plan has been communicated, neither is sanction, and neither measures hidden use.
2. **Support goes with use**
   - Gallup's 36% (May 2026) and 1.7x.
   - The caveat, in our words: "a survey link, not proof that manager support causes use."
   - No opt-in vs mandate study.
3. **Real pilots already cover several uses**
   - Dillon et al.'s tool covered email, meetings and writing (data from 2023 to 2024).
   - Kemp (2026), Gallup's task mix of 51%, 49% and 39%. Its fieldwork ran May 6 to 20, 2026, ending on the cutoff.
   - Pennsylvania: 175 volunteers from 14 agencies, use mix 36%, 27% and 13%. The 95 minutes is self-reported. Labeled data from 2024 to 2025, one tool, OpenAI-led trainings, "did not eliminate, human input".
4. **Results differ by use**
   - Email fell and meetings didn't (Dillon et al., in press, p. 8; data from 2023 to 2024).
   - Patent drafting (Autor et al., 2026): about 10 fewer minutes against a 110-minute mean. Quality rose while lawyers used AI, more for juniors. On a later task without AI, only seniors kept an edge. Labeled US randomized trial, not peer-reviewed, data from May 2025 to February 2026, Google-funded with Google staff among the authors.
   - Note-taking strain (Olson et al.): labeled data from 2024.
   - Customer service agents (Wang et al., 2026): shorter chats, but "substantially lowers ratings". Labeled not US (Taobao, China), preprint, data from August 2024, an Alibaba author.
   - Average time saved: 2.2% (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED Blog, 2026). Labeled self-reported, data Q3 2024 to Q2 2026.
5. **Time the work rather than ask people**
   - Yu et al. (2026): predicted faster, no real difference, lower felt effort. Labeled preprint, simple lab tasks, collection dates not stated.
   - FRED's own self-report caveat.
6. **What the evidence does not show, and where it is thin**
   - Counterpoints from Addendum 7:
     - saved time may not show up in output (Dillon);
     - delegating more meant more invalid work (Ma et al., 2026; preprint, 20 professionals, Microsoft authors, collection dates not stated);
     - speed can cost quality (Wang);
     - help is uneven and can fade without AI (Autor).
   - Gaps, in Addendum 7's wording:
     - one randomized field trial of agents, outside the US and in customer service only;
     - no opt-in pilot study, and none that ran several tools;
     - teams remain an open question (Pennsylvania p. 54);
     - much of the field data above was collected before 2026.
7. **Design the pilot**
   - Use cases, each with a tool, an owner and a baseline (ours).
   - A spending limit in each tool (ours).
   - Volunteers rest on Pennsylvania's "not a representative sample" (p. 12).
   - Review first, read-only, a named reviewer and an off-limits list (ours).
8. **The stop rule**
   - GOV.UK's "not a failure to stop" and 4 to 8 weeks for a discovery phase (GDS, 2021, exempt).
   - Five measures, judged per use case at baseline, week 2 and week 4. Bars labeled ours. Source line in Addendum 7's wording.
   - Customer ratings are flagged as an outcome to watch in customer-facing uses (Wang).
   - Pennsylvania's use-falling-off and data-worry quotes, labeled data from 2024 to 2025.
   - End-date decision (ours).
9. **Takeaway** — Addendum 7's replacement line on the single agent field trial (Wang et al., 2026, data from 2024); five asks.

## Close / call to action
Leaders should be obligated to decide, not to stay silent. Five asks:
1. Sanction, said out loud.
2. Use cases, each with a tool, an owner and a baseline.
3. A spending limit in each tool.
4. A named reviewer, an off-limits list and read-only access first.
5. The five-measure stop rule, checked at week 2 and week 4, agreed before day one.
