# Outline — Don't wait for the company rollout: a capped, four-week opt-in for Grok team bots

- **Hook:** A short contrast, then the stakes. In the US, 86% of workers whose employer encourages generative AI say they use it for their job, compared with 13% of those whose employer does not (claim #13a; Bick, Blandin and Deming, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis). Yet half of US workers say their employer neither encourages nor discourages AI chatbots (claim #13b; Lin and Parker, Pew Research Center).
- **Pivot (one rhetorical question):** If silence is the norm and encouragement goes with far more use, what can a cautious leader do while the company-wide rollout is still being planned?
- **Promise / purpose:** "The purpose of this article is" to propose a sanctioned, capped, four-week opt-in. Secondly, it answers the "not worth the cost" objection with official list prices, a cap built from real settings, a stop rule, an honest way to weigh cost against time saved, and a fair way to choose who joins. It also shows where Grok Bot fits next to tools a company may already have.

## Close / call to action
Framed as leadership's responsibility: leaders should sanction one bounded pilot rather than leave use to chance. If the company already has Microsoft 365 Copilot or Gemini, the pilot tests what that tool does not cover, not a replacement. Ask for five things: sanction for one pilot team workspace, a fixed seat count and tier, a monthly additional usage limit, an end date four weeks out, and a stop rule plus off-limits list agreed before day one. Invite security and commercial review first (claim #19). Stopping is a legitimate result (claim #9).
