# Outline — Don't wait for the company rollout: a capped, four-week opt-in for Grok team bots

- **Hook:** Your people are not waiting for the rollout. In Denmark, about 41% of workers whose employers offered no encouragement, no enterprise chatbot and no training had still used AI chatbots at work (claim #13). The real choice is not bots or no bots. It is sanctioned use or unsanctioned use.
- **Promise:** A one-page plan an engineering manager can hand to a cautious exec this week: official list prices, a spend cap built from settings the product actually has, a stop rule written before day one, an honest way to weigh cost against time saved, and a fair way to pick who joins.

## Sections
1. Why a sanctioned opt-in beats waiting (not why it beats a mandate) — claims: #13, #14, #15, #16; counterpoint 6 (used: ask leadership to sanction and encourage it)
2. What it costs, in official list prices — claims: #1, #2, #3, #4
3. Make the cap real: two settings, one boundary — claims: #5, #6, #7; counterpoint 4 (used: the cap is only as real as the settings)
4. Write the stop rule before you start — claims: #8, #9
5. Weigh cost against time saved without fooling yourself — claims: #10, #11, #18; counterpoint 3 (used: self-reported savings are weak evidence)
6. Pick who joins: volunteers plus a few who didn't raise a hand — claims: #12, #15, #17; counterpoint 2 (used: opt-in skews who benefits)
7. Clear review first, and keep some work off the bot — claims: #7, #19; counterpoint 1 (used: central rollouts do security and procurement better), counterpoint 5 (used: some work should stay off a bot)
8. How it runs day to day: a structural example — claims: #20

All six counterpoints used; none dropped.

## Close / call to action
Ask the exec for five things, not a company-wide decision: sanction for one pilot team workspace, a fixed seat count and tier, a monthly additional usage limit, an end date four weeks out, and a stop rule they agree to before day one. Invite security and commercial review first (claim #19). Stopping at the end is a legitimate result (claim #9).
